# Copy & Voice Guide

The voice rules from [`BRAND-GUIDE.md`](BRAND-GUIDE.md), expanded into specific writing patterns. If you're drafting marketing copy, outbound, sales decks, or in-product strings, use this as a quick reference.

---

## The voice in one sentence

> Quiet, exact, confident — never effusive, never salesy. State the result, name the system, move on.

---

## Voice principles

### Specific over abstract

| ❌ | ✅ |
|---|---|
| "Transformative business outcomes" | "Quote turnaround compressed from days to minutes" |
| "Significant cost savings" | "30–60% reduction in cost-to-serve" |
| "Industry-leading AI capabilities" | "Pre-call account briefs for every meeting" |

Numbers. Durations. System names. Integrations. These are our texture.

### Stating, not selling

| ❌ | ✅ |
|---|---|
| "Discover how AlienAI can transform your business!" | "AlienAI builds production AI systems for the work modern businesses run on." |
| "Unlock the power of cutting-edge AI." | "Higher order intelligence." |
| "Schedule a demo today!" | "aliens@alienai.llc" |

Declaratives. Period at the end. No exclamation points outside of conversation.

### Honest about scope

State what we **don't** do as often as what we do. The first thing a prospect should learn is that we are not the right firm for half of what they might ask.

> What we don't do: AI strategy decks. Generic chatbots. Custom builds-from-scratch.

This is not modesty — it is positioning.

## Vocabulary

### Words we use

- **System** — what we sell. Not "solution," not "platform," not "tool."
- **Deploy** — what we do with a system. Not "implement."
- **Configure** — how we tailor. Not "customize."
- **Connect** — how we integrate. Use when "integrate" would feel heavy.
- **Production** — when meant. Not as a buzzword.
- **Quietly** — a signature adverb. "Quietly engineered," "quietly compounds."
- **Operate** — the verb of the brand. We ship things that operate.
- **Run** — the partner verb. "Built once. Configured to your stack. Deployed to run."

### Words we don't use

#### Marketing buzzwords (banned)

- ❌ Revolutionary, disruptive, game-changing, transformative
- ❌ Cutting-edge (in marketing copy — show, don't tell)
- ❌ Synergy, leverage, unlock, empower
- ❌ World-class, best-in-class, industry-leading
- ❌ Bleeding edge, paradigm shift, next-generation

#### Agency-speak (banned)

- ❌ Solutions (in marketing copy — use "systems")
- ❌ Custom (we don't sell custom — see Naming below)
- ❌ Bespoke, tailored from scratch
- ❌ The team, our company, the firm
- ❌ "We're excited to," "We're thrilled to," "We're proud to"

#### Cliché AI vocabulary (banned)

- ❌ "Powered by AI," "AI-driven," "AI-enabled"
- ❌ "Harness the power of AI"
- ❌ "AI strategy," "AI transformation," "AI journey"
- ❌ "Generative AI" as a marketing line (specific use is fine)

## Naming

### Systems

When introducing a productized system, use:

> **"The AI [Function] System"**

Examples:
- The AI Recruiting System
- The AI Customer Operations System
- The AI Document Intelligence System
- The AI Sales Automation System
- The AI Knowledge Copilot System
- The AI Workflow Engine

The definite article ("the") signals a singular, named product, not a category. Avoid generic ("Recruiting AI"), toy-sounding (".ai" or "-o" suffixes), or sub-brand creep ("AlienAI Recruit").

### Engagements vs. deployments

- ✅ "Deployment" — we deploy systems
- ✅ "Engagement" — sparingly, for the relationship
- ❌ "Project," "build" — too custom-coded
- ❌ "Implementation" — too SAP

## Punctuation

- **Periods on headline declaratives.** "Higher order intelligence." not "Higher order intelligence"
- **Em-dashes** for parenthetical clauses. Use them deliberately — like this — and never as a crutch.
- **En-dash** for ranges. "30–60%" not "30-60%."
- **No Oxford comma** unless ambiguity would result. We lean against.
- **No exclamation points** in headlines, body copy, or buttons. Allowed in conversational outbound where it would sound robotic to omit.
- **Curly quotes,** always. `you're` not `you\'re`; "the work" not `\"the work\"`.

---

## Capitalization

- **Headlines:** sentence case. Capitalize only first word and proper nouns.
- **Eyebrows:** `UPPERCASE` (rendered via CSS, written in sentence case in JSX where possible).
- **Buttons:** sentence case.
- **System names:** Title Case ("The AI Recruiting System").
- **"AI":** always two capital letters.

---

## Outbound + email

- **Subject lines:** ≤ 8 words. Lowercase except proper nouns. No emoji. No re:re:re:.
- **Opening line:** make it specific to the recipient — never "Hope you're well!"
- **Body:** 3–5 short paragraphs maximum. Plain text preferred.
- **CTA:** one. Either a question they can answer in a sentence, or a "would 15 minutes next Thursday work?"
- **Sign-off:** A first name. Not "Best," not "Cheers," not "Warm regards." Just the name.

### Email signature template

```
Steven Chen
AlienAI LLC
aliens@alienai.llc
```

That's it. No phone block. No LinkedIn QR. No "Schedule a call" link. No company tagline. The negative space is the brand.

## Common scenarios

### Describing a system

> The AI Recruiting System sources, screens, schedules, and onboards candidates at scale. Same engine, configured to your hiring bar, your tone, and your stack. We plug it into your ATS, calendar, and inbox; it handles the rest.

### Quoting a result

> Quote turnaround compressed from days to minutes. Win-rate on quotes responded to within 1 hour rose meaningfully. Reclaimed roughly two full-time desks worth of hours per week.

### Explaining the model

> We build production AI systems and license them as SaaS. The engine is the same across customers. The configuration is yours. Each system is plumbed into your stack, trained on your reality, and kept running by us.

### Saying no

> This sounds like a custom build from scratch — not the shape of work we're set up for. If it ever becomes a productized motion, we'd be the right team. For now, we'd recommend a boutique consultancy.

(Real, declined, honest — the AlienAI way.)
